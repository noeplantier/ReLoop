# Evidence for the need (for the submission form and the pitch)

Every point below was read in the primary source on 15 Nov 2026; page numbers are given so anyone can check.
Paraphrased, not copied. What we have NOT evidenced is listed at the end: say it as openly as the rest.

## 1. Built event elements are a major, addressable emissions source

- For most events, attendee travel is the largest single source of emissions. For events with a large built
  element (trade shows, exhibitions), **"space design and production" (stands and other fit-out) is normally
  the second biggest contributor after travel**.
  Source: Net Zero Carbon Events, *A Net Zero Roadmap for the Events Industry*, Nov 2022, section 1.1.3, p. 11.
- In French events-industry data reproduced there (Unimev), once travel and accommodation are excluded, space
  design and production is the largest category for every event type shown except corporate events, where
  food dominates (Figure 1.1.4, p. 12; values read from a chart, so quote it qualitatively, not as a %).
- The same roadmap ranks actions: **avoid, then reduce (including reuse), then replace, and only last offset**
  (Figure 1.1.2, p. 11). Reusing existing equipment is a "reduce" action, ahead of offsetting.

Why it matters for ReLoop: travel is outside an organiser's control at booking time; equipment sourcing is a
decision they make for every event, which is exactly where ReLoop sits.

## 2. The events industry's own roadmap targets reuse of exactly this equipment

- Action Area 2 ("redesign events to utilise sustainable materials and be waste free") says event
  infrastructure is typically designed for short-term use and that producing and disposing of items is a
  significant part of an event's carbon footprint (p. 23).
- It describes the circular alternative with the example of **using and reusing a venue's tables and chairs
  instead of buying new ones**, and of renting booths instead of building and tearing them down (p. 23).
- Milestones: **by 2030, at least 50% of items hired, repurposed, refurbished or reused**; by 2050 all assets
  and materials hired, repurposed, refurbished, recycled or re-used, and zero waste to landfill (pathway
  table, p. 24).
- The initiative was launched in 2021, presented its roadmap at COP27 (UNFCCC programme), and reports 400+
  supporting organisations from 55 countries (p. 4).

Why it matters: ReLoop is a tool for the 2030 milestone (more items hired/reused instead of new), not a
new idea we invented the need for.

## 3. Organisers are under pressure from their audiences

- UFI research cited in the roadmap: 73% of exhibitors and 71% of visitors say it is important that a trade
  show shows a strong commitment to sustainability; 34% of exhibitors and 36% of visitors would not attend a
  show without a reasonable approach to sustainability (p. 9, citing UFI, July 2021).

## 4. What happens to furniture at end of life (context, United States)

- US EPA: furniture and furnishings in municipal solid waste were **12.1 million tons in 2018**; **80.1% was
  landfilled**, 19.5% combusted with energy recovery, and measurable recycling was about 40 thousand tons
  (about 0.3%). Source: US EPA, *Durable Goods: Product-Specific Data* (Facts and Figures about Materials,
  Waste and Recycling), data 1960-2018.
- Scope warning: household furniture in the US, not event equipment and not Türkiye. Use it only as context
  for "new furniture usually ends up in landfill", never as an events or Antalya figure.

## 5. Carbon factors used in the product

- UK Government GHG Conversion Factors for Company Reporting 2025 (DESNZ/Defra), Open Government Licence v3.0:
  material production per tonne and road freight per tonne-km. Details: `GET /impact/methodology`.

## What we have NOT evidenced (say so)

- No data on how much event equipment is bought new versus rented or reused in Antalya or Türkiye.
- No interviews or survey of Antalya organisers or venues yet. The pilot (2-3 venues, real inventory, real
  requests) is the proposed next validation step precisely to measure this.
- No figure for the carbon footprint of the events industry as a whole; we do not quote one.

## Slide-ready version (3 lines)

  exhibitions (Net Zero Carbon Events roadmap, 2022).
